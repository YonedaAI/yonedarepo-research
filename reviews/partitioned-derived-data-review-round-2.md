# Peer Review: *Partitioned Derived Data (Part II of Source Control as a Data System)*

## 1. Overall Evaluation

This paper develops a formal model of partitioned derived data over immutable repository snapshots and applies it to audit YonedaRepo at commit `cfa4845`. Its central abstraction—*key sufficiency* ($\kappa(p,x)=\kappa(p',x') \implies f(p,x)=f(p',x')$)—cleanly unifies content-addressed partition reuse, parent-recursive derivation folds, commit-keyed history memoization, and partition-level retry for write jobs. The paper's greatest strength is its intellectual honesty: it uses its own formalism to uncover two real correctness defects in the system under study (an insufficient bare-tree-id key for search segments that misattributes paths and violates read grants after directory renames, and write skew in partitioned codemods that omit read sets) and confirms the first defect with an executable Rust integration test.

1. **Mathematical correctness**: The core theorems (Theorem 5.2, Proposition 5.7, Theorem 10.2, Proposition 10.3, Proposition 10.4) are sound. However, three propositions have unstated preconditions or incomplete case analyses: Proposition 9.3 ignores the 20,000-file partition cap when a codemod is scoped to a subdirectory $q/$ of a top-level partition; Proposition 11.1 applies the uncoalesced landing rate $\lambda_c$ to partition rebuilds while applying the coalesced rate $\tilde\lambda_c$ to lookups without assuming that coalesced commits touch disjoint partitions; and Proposition 11.2 omits single-file partitions from its proof and leaves the partitioning rule undefined when the root tree itself has at most $g$ files.
2. **Clarity**: Definitions and proofs are mostly tight and easy to verify. A few formal definitions omit edge cases that later proofs rely on (such as how $\mathrm{cov}(A)$ acts on prefix entries `dir/` and which edge direction Step 2 of the planner traverses).
3. **Completeness**: Within the scope of a theoretical and code-analytic paper, the coverage of read jobs, write jobs, history folds, semantic planning, and garbage collection (`vacuum`) is thorough. As the paper candidly acknowledges in Sections 1, 13, and 15, empirical performance evaluation is nearly absent—no segment build times, R2 lookup latencies, segment sizes, or codemod/planner durations are measured, leaving the granularity optimization in Proposition 11.1 uncalibrated.
4. **Logical structure**: The progression from immutable Merkle trees (Section 2) and the job model (Sections 3–4) to key sufficiency (Section 5), read jobs and the prefix counterexample (Sections 6–7), history folds (Section 8), write jobs (Section 9), semantic staging (Section 10), partition granularity (Section 11), and maintenance/evidence (Sections 12–13) is logical and cohesive.
5. **LaTeX quality**: The manuscript compiles cleanly and uses environments and tables effectively, but it has systematic copy-editing flaws in the bibliography (every page range uses a hyphen `-` instead of an en-dash `--`), inconsistent macros for file names (`\code{...}` vs. `\path{...}`), missing non-breaking spaces before units (`1 MiB`, `8.1 s`), an unused `cleveref` import, and mixed British/American spelling (`analyse`/`behaviour` vs. `color`/`serializable`).
6. **Prose quality (House Style)**: The paper's short paragraphs and theorem proofs are crisp, direct, and free of nominalization. However, several longer commentary paragraphs following formal results violate the house style by packing three or four distinct claims into a single paragraph, opening with meta-commentary or signposting (*"Two features stand out"*, *"The batch condition is not a formality"*, *"Two points of precision matter when the theorem is used"*), and placing operational mechanics before the main finding.

## 2. Structured Feedback by Severity

### Critical Issues

None. No fatal mathematical or methodological flaw invalidates the paper's core contributions.

### Major Issues

1. **Unstated partition-boundary and size-cap precondition in Proposition 9.3 (Serializable codemods, lines 521–528)**
   - **Issue**: Proposition 9.3 states that if a codemod over version $v$ is restricted to a directory prefix $q/$ and declares $\R=\{q/\}$, then the landed tree below $q/$ equals the codemod applied to the snapshot immediately before its landing. The proof (line 527) argues that because no entry after $v$ touched a path below $q/$, *"the subtree at $q/$ is the same immediately before the landing as at $v$. The codemod applied to that snapshot computes the same candidates and the same changes."*
   - **Why this fails in the implemented system**: In YonedaRepo, partitions are *top-level* directories $d/$, and any partition with more than 20,000 files (`MAX_PARTITION_FILES`, lines 160, 517) is skipped in its entirety. Suppose $q/$ is a proper subdirectory of a top-level directory $d/$ (for example, $q/ = \code{src/sub/}$ inside $d/ = \code{src/}$). A concurrent commit landing after $v$ that adds files under $\code{src/other/}$ does not overlap $\R = \{\code{src/sub/}\}$ and passes backward validation, yet it can push the total file count of $\code{src/}$ above 20,000. On the snapshot immediately before landing, the codemod skips the entire $\code{src/}$ partition and makes no changes under $q/$, whereas at version $v$ it rewrote $q/$.
   - **Fix**: State explicitly in Proposition 9.3 either that $q/$ is a top-level directory or that its enclosing top-level partition does not cross the 20,000-file indexing threshold between $v$ and landing (or state Proposition 9.3 for the un-truncated formal model of Definition 9.1 and note the 20,000-file caveat alongside line 517).

2. **Incomplete partitioning rule and missing single-file case in Proposition 11.2 (Pruning under size-bounded partitions, lines 656–665)**
   - **Issue**: Line 656 defines the recursive partitioning rule: *"Make a directory $r$ a partition root when its subtree has at most $g$ files and its parent's has more, and treat a file whose parent has more than $g$ files as a partition of its own."* This definition and the proof of Proposition 11.2 (line 664) have two gaps:
     1. If the root tree $\varepsilon$ itself has at most $g$ files, $\varepsilon$ has no parent directory, so under the literal rule no directory has a parent with more than $g$ files and no partition is emitted. The rule must specify that the root directory $\varepsilon$ is a partition root when the repository has at most $g$ files.
     2. The proof of Proposition 11.2 (line 664) considers only directory partitions (*"Whether a directory $r$ is a partition root depends only on the file counts of $r$ and of its parent. ... Take a partition of $c$ rooted at $r$ with neither $r$ nor its parent in $A$."*) and omits single-file partitions. A single file $f$ whose parent directory $p$ has $>g$ files is a partition of $c$; if its key is not a key of $c'$, then either $f \in \W_v$ (so $p \in A$ and $f$ is a child of $p \in A$) or $f \notin \W_v$ and $p$'s file count crossed $g$ between $c'$ and $c$ (so again $p \in A$ and $f$ is a child of $p \in A$).
   - **Fix**: Add the root-tree base case to the definition on line 656 and include the two-sentence verification for single-file partitions in the proof on line 664.

3. **Inconsistent treatment of job coalescing between rebuilds and lookups in Proposition 11.1 (Optimal partition size, lines 639–650)**
   - **Issue**: Proposition 11.1 distinguishes the landing arrival rate $\lambda_c$ from the coalesced derivation job rate $\tilde\lambda_c \le \lambda_c$. It uses $\tilde\lambda_c$ in the lookup term $b = (\tilde\lambda_c + \lambda_q)N c_\ell$, but uses the uncoalesced landing rate $\lambda_c$ in the rebuild term $a = \lambda_c u c_i$, arguing (line 649) that *"Each landing causes $u$ partitions of $g$ files to be rebuilt, costing $u\,g\,c_i$, whichever job builds them."*
   - **Why this requires an explicit hypothesis**: Section 3 (line 164) explains that coalescing occurs when a burst of landings moves a ref before the Queue consumer runs, so the consumer derives only the latest head. If two landings in a coalesced burst modify the *same* partition (and no query is pinned to the intermediate commit), that partition is rebuilt once for the burst, not once per landing, making the rebuild rate strictly less than $\lambda_c u$. The formula $a = \lambda_c u c_i$ holds only if coalesced landings touch disjoint sets of partitions (or if $u$ is defined as the average number of newly dirtied partitions per landing after coalescing).
   - **Fix**: State explicitly in Proposition 11.1 that coalesced landings within a burst are assumed to modify disjoint partitions (or replace $\lambda_c u$ by $\tilde\lambda_c \tilde u$, the rate of distinct rebuilt partitions).

4. **Conflation of characters and UTF-8 bytes, and incomplete emptiness condition, in Proposition 6.1 (Candidate superset, lines 390–397)**
   - **Issue**: Proposition 6.1 states: *"Let $q$ be a query of 2 to 200 characters... If the case-folded content of $F$ contains the case-folded $q$, then $F$ is among the candidates..."* The proof (line 396) then writes: *"The code folds ASCII case in both the content and the query and extracts the set of three-byte windows that contain no newline. If the folded query has no such window, which happens for two-character queries, the content filter is absent..."*
   - **Why this is imprecise**: In Rust (`search.rs`), strings are UTF-8 byte sequences and ASCII case folding (`to_ascii_lowercase`) operates on bytes. A two-character query containing multibyte UTF-8 code points has 4 to 8 bytes and therefore yields one to six three-byte windows. Conversely, a query of 3 or more ASCII bytes that contains a newline at least every third byte (such as `"ab\ncd"`) also has no newline-free three-byte window.
   - **Fix**: Specify in Proposition 6.1 whether the 2-to-200 limit is measured in bytes or Unicode scalar values, and in line 396 write *"which happens when every contiguous newline-free slice of the folded query is shorter than three bytes (in particular, for any two-byte query)"*.

5. **Under-specified definitions and omitted qualifications in Proposition 5.6 (lines 312–322) and Section 10 (lines 554–565, 597)**
   - **Proposition 5.6 (lines 312–322)**: The statement applies to $\Ch(c',c)$, which Definition 5.4 defines for an arbitrary key function $\kappa$, but the proof (line 321) invokes the specific implemented keys $\id(\tree(c)|_p)$ and $\code{files-}\,\|\,H(\lambda(F))$. Because $p \notin \Pfx(\W_v)$ implies that the partition pair $(p,x) \in \Pi(c)$ belongs identically to $\Pi(c')$, $\kappa(p,x) \in \kappa(\Pi(c'))$ holds immediately for *any* deterministic function $\kappa$ of $(p,x)$ without appealing to the specific digest formula.
   - **Definition 10.1 and Planner Steps 1–2 (lines 554–565)**: Definition 10.1 (line 556) defines $\mathrm{cov}(A)$ for path entries and symbol entries, omitting directory prefix entries (`dir/`), even though work items may contain prefix entries. Step 1 (line 561) uses *"writes a node of it"* and *"touch it"* without defining them as $\mathrm{cov}(\W_i)$ and $\mathrm{cov}(\R_i \cup \W_i)$, and Step 2 (line 562) says *"reachable from its covered nodes within $d$ reference steps"* without stating whether reachability follows outgoing edges in $G_s$ from $\mathrm{cov}(\R_i \cup \W_i)$ or $\mathrm{cov}(\R_i)$.
   - **Feed-order qualification (line 597)**: Line 597 states that *"Step 3 admits only orders that place a writer before the items it feeds"*, omitting the qualification correctly stated in Theorem 10.2(d) (line 574) that this holds only when the writer and reader lie in *different strongly connected components* of the feed relation.

6. **Multi-claim paragraphs and signposting that violate the house style (lines 221, 324, 429, 493–496, 530, 586, 630)**
   - Several paragraphs bundle multiple distinct claims or open with meta-commentary (*"Two features stand out"*, *"The batch condition is not a formality"*, *"Two points of precision matter when the theorem is used"*). See Section 3 below for exact quotes of the three weakest paragraphs and a model rewrite.

## 3. Weakest Three Paragraphs and Model Rewrite

### Weakest Paragraph 1 (Line 324)

> The proof uses only that every changed path is touched. The converse also holds in the code, because \code{prepare.rs} skips a requested change whose new mode and blob equal the old ones, but the proposition does not need it. The inclusion can be strict. A touched directory whose new content equals some other partition of $c'$ is not changed in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:pdd:changed}. The implementation does not use $\W_v$ at all; it looks up every key, which is cheap when the root has few entries because listing the root costs one tree read. Pruning by touched paths becomes useful only with deeper partitionings, where listing every partition key costs one tree read per internal directory. The proposition relies on partition boundaries that are fixed by the layout: a top-level directory is a partition whatever its size. Section~\ref{sec:pdd:granularity} shows what changes when boundaries depend on size.

- **Critique against house style**: This paragraph contains four unrelated claims: (1) whether `prepare.rs` satisfies the converse of the touched-path hypothesis, (2) why the inclusion in Proposition 5.6 can be strict, (3) why the current implementation looks up all keys instead of pruning by $\W_v$, and (4) that the proposition assumes layout-fixed boundaries (ending with pure signposting to Section 11). It lacks a single governing claim and reads as a list of marginal notes.

### Weakest Paragraph 2 (Line 429)

> For a codemod, the consequence is a refusal or a wrong reduce. After the rename, the codemod produces a change for each matching \code{src/\ldots} path with an expected old blob. The parent tree has no such path, so commit preparation rejects the whole codemod with \code{CONFLICT} (\code{prepare.rs}), and it does so on every retry because the stored segment never changes. With duplicated directories, the reduce receives two identical changes for each matching path under one prefix and none under the other; either the commit is refused or one directory is left unmodified. No test in the repository covers either case. We executed the core of the counterexample with a test written against the Rust code at \code{cfa4845} and run outside the repository (Appendix~\ref{app:pdd:prefixtest}). For a repository with identical top-level directories \code{a/} and \code{b/}, the test asserts that the partition plan gives both the same key and that the segments built for them list \code{a/lib.rs} and \code{b/lib.rs} respectively; for a rename of \code{src/} to \code{lib/}, it asserts the same pair of facts. It also asserts that stripping each segment's prefix makes the two segments equal, which is the content of repair (b) below. The test passes. The consequences for search responses and codemods follow from the TypeScript adapter, which uses the plan's key unchanged as the R2 key (Listing~\ref{lst:pdd:key}); we traced them by reading that code and did not run the adapter.

- **Critique against house style**: This 10-sentence paragraph merges two distinct subjects: the failure mode of write jobs (codemods) under prefix-free keys, and the experimental verification of the entire prefix counterexample (which tests search planning and indexing in Rust, previews repair (b), and qualifies what was traced by inspection in TypeScript). It also contains forward signposting (*"which is the content of repair (b) below"*) and abrupt telegraphic sentences (*"The test passes."*).

### Weakest Paragraph 3 (Line 530)

> The batch condition is not a formality. Part~I shows that the combiner at commit \code{cfa4845} validates batch members against the log but not against each other, so two members can each write what the other read (Part~I, Theorem~7.3). A codemod with declared reads that shares a batch with a commit adding an occurrence of the find string in a scanned file would land, and the new occurrence would survive. The restriction to one directory prefix is necessary. A repository-wide codemod scans every top-level directory and the root files, and a declared set of those directory prefixes and file paths does not cover a top-level directory or root file added after $v$. A concurrent commit that adds such a file containing the find string writes nothing the codemod declared, validation returns a rebase, and the new occurrence survives. Part~I's access entries have no form that covers the whole repository, so the only condition that makes a repository-wide codemod serializable is that its landing be a fast-forward, with no entry on the ref after $v$. The cost of declaring reads, in either form, is more refusals: any concurrent landing in scope forces the codemod to rerun, which is the only way to rewrite a new occurrence.

- **Critique against house style**: This paragraph packs three distinct arguments into one block: (1) why intra-batch validation is required (Part I, Theorem 7.3), (2) why a repository-wide codemod cannot be serialized via declared read sets (because the access language cannot express a repository-wide wildcard that catches newly added root files or top-level directories), and (3) the throughput cost of declaring read sets (forced reruns on concurrent writes). Moreover, it opens with meta-commentary (*"The batch condition is not a formality"*) instead of stating the claim directly.

### Model Rewrite of Weakest Paragraph 2 (Line 429)

To adhere to the house style—one identifiable claim per paragraph, findings stated directly before qualification, and no signposting—the codemod failure claim and the experimental verification claim in Line 429 should be separated, with the codemod paragraph written as follows (and the test verification placed in its own paragraph immediately after):

> Prefix-free search keys cause a codemod either to abort with a false conflict or to leave duplicate directories unedited. After a directory rename from \code{src/} to \code{lib/}, the codemod reads the stale segment and emits changes for \code{src/\ldots} paths that no longer exist in the parent tree; commit preparation in \code{prepare.rs} therefore rejects the commit with \code{CONFLICT} on every attempt because the cached segment is never invalidated. When two top-level directories have identical content, the reduce step receives two identical edits for the prefix whose segment was stored last and none for the other, leaving the second directory untouched.
>
> An integration test against the Rust crates at commit \code{cfa4845} confirms the underlying key collision (Appendix~\ref{app:pdd:prefixtest}), while the downstream effects on search and codemods follow by inspection of the TypeScript adapter. Given a commit with identical directories \code{a/} and \code{b/}, or a rename of \code{src/} to \code{lib/}, \code{search\_plan} assigns both partitions the same key while \code{index\_partition} emits segments with distinct path prefixes; stripping those prefixes makes the segments identical. Because \code{repo-derived.ts} passes the plan's key directly to R2 (Listing~\ref{lst:pdd:key}), both partitions map to the same storage object.
